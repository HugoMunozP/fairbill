How to lower your internet bill: a 10-minute call script

Internet companies raise prices on loyal customers once promotions end. Most will lower your bill if you ask the right way, with the right numbers.

Updated November 2026.

Before you call: get three numbers

  1. What you pay now for internet only, from your latest bill.
  2. What your provider advertises to new customers for the same speed. See advertised prices by provider.
  3. What your neighbors pay for the same plan in your ZIP code. Check it on Fairbill.

The script

Hi, I'm calling about my internet bill. I'm paying [your price] a month for [your plan].

Your website advertises this plan for [advertised price] to new customers, and people in my ZIP code typically pay [neighbors' price].

I'd like to stay, but I need my bill closer to that. What can you do for me today?

If they say no: "Then please transfer me to the retention department. I'm ready to cancel."

On Fairbill, the script fills in your real numbers automatically.

If they won't lower it

  • Ask for the retention (cancellations) department. They usually have more room to offer discounts.
  • Ask about a slower plan. Many homes don't need more than 300 to 500 Mbps.
  • Return rented equipment if you can use your own router, and remove add-ons you don't use.
  • Check other providers at your address. Some of the lowest advertised prices right now:
  • Spectrum Internet Advantage (100 Mbps): $30/mo advertised
  • Spectrum Internet Premier (500 Mbps): $40/mo advertised
  • Xfinity 300 Mbps: $40/mo advertised
  • T-Mobile Fiber Fiber 300 (300 Mbps): $40/mo advertised
  • Verizon Fios Fios 300 Mbps: $50/mo advertised

Questions

What is the retention department?

It's the team that handles customers who want to cancel. They usually have more discounts available than the first agent you reach.

When is the best time to call?

A few weeks before your promotional price ends, or right after a price increase shows up on your bill.

Will asking for a lower price affect my service?

No. Asking about prices and discounts doesn't change your service unless you agree to a new plan.
